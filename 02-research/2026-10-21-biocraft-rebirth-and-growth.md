# BioCraft rebirth: Bioethics League

Decision memo • researched October 21, 2026 • planning horizon: October 2026–October 2027

**Recommendation:** Reposition BioCraft as **Bioethics League**, a student-led school network offering a free, short bioethics challenge, public student case briefs, and an annual showcase. Retain DebateCraft’s administrative support initially. Treat Exeter Biology Symposium (EBS) as a potential program partner with a defined contribution. Build independent school relationships and a recognizable body of work.

This is a research recommendation, not an approved implementation specification. No website changes, registrations, outreach, purchases, or partnership commitments were made.

- **Question type:** comparison and strategic recommendation, including naming and positioning.
- **User-provided:** four staff; currently a DebateCraft subprogram; concern that connection to EBS limits staff growth. The primary objective is recognition, partnerships, and visibility within one year.

- **Important limitation:** these are current workspace facts, including work in progress, not confirmation of the deployed website. Two retrieval methods could not fetch DebateCraft’s public biology page. No enrollment, completion, budget, analytics, existing partner agreements, or staff availability records were supplied.
- **Public evidence:** EBS describes a dry-lab research pathway culminating on April 25, 2027. Bioethics could complement that pathway; a partnership is not established by this research. [EBS](https://exeterbiosymposium.vercel.app/about.html)

## Choose a model that fits the objective

The following is strategic judgment, not a measured forecast.

| Option | Likely first-year benefit | Constraint | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expand the current small classes | Good direct service and testimonials | Staff teaching time grows with enrollment; limited public artifacts | Retain one small pilot cohort as a testing ground |
| Become an EBS department | Access to an existing event and community, if agreed | Public credit, timing, and decisions may remain concentrated in EBS | Accept defined collaboration, not exclusive dependence |
| Start a broad biology research nonprofit | Broad positioning | Duplicates EBS; research mentoring and quality control exceed demonstrated capacity | Do not prioritize |
| Launch a large standalone competition immediately | A visible launch | Unknown demand, judge burden, and a new award with little recognition | Pilot before promising scale |
| Build a school network around one repeatable challenge | Schools distribute it; work can be published and attributed; staff own real functions | Needs teacher adoption and reliable external review | **Recommended** |

## The complete repositioning

**Name:** Bioethics League  
**Descriptor:** The student network for biology, ethics, and debate.  
**Tagline:** Debate the future of biology.  
**Mission:** Help students investigate the science, weigh competing values, and defend thoughtful decisions about the future of biology and medicine.

**Public introduction:** “Bioethics League brings secondary-school students together to examine the choices behind advances in biology and medicine. Through free school challenges, evidence-based discussions, and published student briefs, participants learn to make and defend informed judgments.”

**Proposed homepage:**

> Who gets to decide the future of biology?
>
> Explore real dilemmas in genetics, medicine, and biotechnology. Work with your school, exchange ideas with other teams, and publish a reasoned response.
>
> Primary action: Bring the League to your school.  
> Secondary action: Explore the Bioethics Challenge.

Use “A DebateCraft initiative” as a secondary attribution while that remains the actual relationship. A distinct public identity does not require immediate incorporation or a separate administrative organization. List EBS as a collaborator only after agreement, and describe its actual contribution. Avoid implying institutional endorsement from individual affiliations.

Give the program its own calendar, partner contacts, credited outputs, and decisions within an agreed budget. Membership and leadership recruitment should be open beyond Exeter. Preserve continuity through documented work and successor training.

### Why this name

“Bioethics” tells teachers what the program covers. “League” suggests school membership and repeat participation. It fits DebateCraft’s existing strengths while allowing ownership beyond one campus and event. It is descriptive rather than highly inventive; that tradeoff favors initial comprehension. Its technical first word is less immediately familiar to younger students, so the plain-language descriptor matters.

## One flagship offering

**Bioethics Challenge:** a free four-week school program for ages 13–18, initially in English, using existing clubs or classes. Pilot with three schools in one practical scheduling region. A teacher sponsor handles each school’s local sessions. Begin with one theme, such as decisions about gene editing; expand themes only after reuse works.

1. **Week 1 — Understand:** read a short science explainer and case, identify the decision and uncertainty.
2. **Week 2 — Discuss:** hold a local session considering evidence, affected groups, competing values, and counterarguments.
3. **Week 3 — Exchange:** share arguments or recorded presentations with another school; revise in response. Asynchronous exchange makes distant time zones manageable.
4. **Week 4 — Submit:** produce a 600–800-word team brief and a short presentation. Selected teams receive an expert discussion opportunity; publish only checked, permission-cleared work.

Provide one facilitator guide, a slide deck, a common rubric, a submission template, and a shared office hour. Schools perform the repeat teaching. The central staff train facilitators, answer recurring questions, review a bounded set of outputs, and organize one showcase.

Assess scientific accuracy, ethical reasoning, treatment of uncertainty, and response to objections. A reasoned position can change during discussion; avoid rewarding speaking polish alone. Use external reviewers for finalists and manage conflicts where staff coach entrants. Do not present ordinary student briefs as peer-reviewed research.

Retain the existing ten-session course only if it supports pilot testing or facilitator training. Do not promise weekly central teaching, a research fellowship, a journal, a large tournament circuit, and a daily content schedule simultaneously.

## Partnerships and recognition

Prioritize a small concrete request over a request for general endorsement.

| Priority | Prospect type | First request | What it receives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm school biology/debate teachers reached through existing members | Run the four-week pilot with one group and a named sponsor | Ready materials, an interschool exchange, and student work to showcase |
| 2 | EBS organizers | Consider one clearly credited ethics workshop before the symposium, with an online fallback | A complementary discussion activity without transferring research oversight |
| 3 | Bioethics educators or medical-student societies | Review one case or attend one 60-minute discussion | Bounded education contribution and accurate attribution |
| 4 | Doctor of Excellence / CUHK bioethics outreach contacts | Explore beginner preparation or a guest session that complements their existing program | Prepared students and reusable outreach evidence |
| 5 | School publications and relevant education newsletters | Cover completed student work, a school exchange, or an agreed collaboration | A concrete story with attributable results |

These are prospects, not confirmed partners. An individual reviewer affiliated with a university is not a university partnership. A delivered activity should precede a logo claim. Never condition expert participation on endorsing the organization.

The distribution loop is: teacher adopts → students produce work → school publishes or references it → another teacher sees a credible example → the next school joins. The public output is the reason to communicate; social posts summarize that output rather than substitute for it.

Do not compete with established bioethics institutions on prestige. Offer them a well-run channel to students. A strong externally credited collaboration can matter more in year one than a self-created award.

### Targets, not forecasts

**Base case by October 2027:**

- 10–12 active schools, with at least half outside Exeter-linked recruitment channels.
- 100–180 unique active students: approximately 10–12 schools × 10–15 learners. Count duplicates across cycles once.
- At least 50% of eligible pilot/early schools repeat or confirm a dated next cycle.
- 2–3 external organizations deliver a defined collaboration; schools are counted separately.
- 20–30 edited team briefs, six reusable cases, and one public annual showcase.
- 5–8 substantive references on independent school, partner, or education-publication pages. Do not count your own reposts as independent recognition.

**Stretch:** 20 schools and approximately 300 active students, only after facilitator delivery and review capacity are proven. This is not a promise of growth or a claim about current reach.

An active school must complete the agreed activities and submit work; a named contact alone does not qualify. Active students must participate in at least two sessions and contribute to a submission. Track impressions and followers separately. Compare branded searches and qualified inquiries against the first month’s baseline; do not invent an absolute SEO target without data.

### Capacity and money assumptions

Assume four staff contribute approximately four hours each per week over 36 active weeks: **576 combined hours**. This is a planning assumption to confirm, not known availability. Allocate roughly 25% to school support, 25% to materials/review, 25% to delivery, 15% to publishing, and 10% to administration and contingency. If actual availability is half that, start with 4–6 schools and three reusable cases.

For 30 briefs, two 20-minute reviews each require 20 reviewer-hours before coordination. One review pass across six cases at two hours each requires another 12 hours. External review capacity must be secured before promising expert feedback; staff time still covers coordination and editing. Cap submissions or run a smaller noncompetitive showcase if reviewers cannot be secured.

An illustrative USD 500–1,500 first-year cash envelope could cover web/email tools, accessible materials, and limited reviewer or event costs if school space and existing tools are contributed. This is not a quote and excludes travel and paid staffing. The pilot should be possible using existing infrastructure. Avoid paid advertising until adoption is demonstrated.

## First 30 days and stop rules

1. Establish the real baseline: past participants, returning learners, current school relationships, staff hours, and accessible channels.
2. Interview teachers about the four-week offer; test the two preferred names. Ask what would stop them running it.
3. Prepare one checked case and a one-page school offer. Secure one sponsor per pilot school and one reviewer before announcing expert involvement.
4. Agree on internal ownership and how DebateCraft and any EBS collaboration will be credited.
5. Schedule three pilots and measure preparation/support time.

If fewer than three schools commit after 15 qualified teacher conversations, revise the offer before expanding branding work. If fewer than two pilots complete, or support needs exceed the time budget, simplify the format before recruiting more schools. If EBS declines, run the same small online program independently. If institutional cohosting proves easier than acquiring schools directly, deliver through that partner with explicit credit and access to program evaluation, rather than forcing a standalone event.

Review active schools, repeat intent, completed collaborations, external references, and staff load monthly. This needs a simple operating review, not an automated competitor monitor yet.
